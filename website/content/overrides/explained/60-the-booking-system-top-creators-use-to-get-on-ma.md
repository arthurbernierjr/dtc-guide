# The Booking System Top Creators Use to Get on Major Podcasts

Brendan Boyd's discussion of podcast booking brings publicity down to a practical matching problem. A guest wants visibility. A host needs a worthwhile conversation. The pitch works when it explains how those interests meet.

A biography alone makes the host do much of the creative work. “I founded a company” could lead to dozens of familiar conversations. “I rebuilt our customer onboarding after discovering why first-time users abandoned it” suggests a story, a problem, and lessons an audience might care about.

## Pitch the conversation, then prepare to deliver it

Start with the show's audience. What do they already know? What problem brings them back? Which part of your experience would add something to the conversations the host already publishes?

Build the pitch around that intersection. Offer a clear topic, a few promising questions, and evidence that you can discuss it concretely. A brief relevant clip can be more useful than a long list of flattering adjectives.

Preparation continues after the booking. Gather examples, check the claims you plan to make, and know which details you cannot share. Leave room for the host's curiosity rather than memorizing a speech.

For someone building a technical career, this is useful even before appearing on a major show. Explaining one project in a way that serves a particular audience is the same skill you need in a portfolio walkthrough, an interview, or a conversation with a potential collaborator.
