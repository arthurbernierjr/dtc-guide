# Why Regulators Are Fighting Back Against DeFi

A smart contract can execute a transaction according to published code. That changes some relationships between users and intermediaries, but it does not remove every dependency. Interfaces, collateral, price feeds, governance decisions, and the conversion between financial systems still matter.

The episode approaches this as a conflict between emerging networks and established institutions. A useful companion question is where each system places trust. Who can change a rule? Who can interrupt a service? Who can explain or remedy an unexpected result?

## Efficiency is one part of the argument

Removing an intermediary can reduce friction in one process while moving responsibility to the user or another technical component. A transaction that executes exactly as coded may still reflect a faulty price, an exploit, or a misunderstanding of the contract.

Regulatory debates also involve competing goals: access, innovation, market integrity, accountability, and protection against loss. You can examine those goals without assuming every intervention has one motive or every protocol has the same design.

Choose one function—lending, exchange, or payment—and draw the complete dependency chain on both sides. That comparison is more informative than treating “banks” and “DeFi” as two uniform teams. It helps you see what the technology changes and which difficult responsibilities remain.
