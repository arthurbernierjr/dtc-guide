# Episode 2: AI, Machine Learning & The Promise of Self Driving Cars (Deeper Than Code)

Driving makes the promise of AI tangible. A vehicle must interpret its surroundings, anticipate what other road users might do, and choose an action within a changing environment. Recognizing an object is only part of that task.

Imagine a pedestrian standing near a crossing. The system needs more than the label “person.” It needs to reason about movement, road conditions, nearby vehicles, and the consequences of its own action. Uncertainty is part of the ordinary scene.

## Name the conditions and the human's role

The phrase “self-driving” can hide important differences. A feature that assists a driver and a system designed to perform the driving task under specified conditions should not be treated as interchangeable. [NHTSA's automated vehicle guidance](https://www.nhtsa.gov/vehicle-safety/automated-vehicle-safety) distinguishes levels of automation and the responsibilities associated with them.

For any demonstration, ask where the system is designed to operate, which conditions are outside that scope, and what happens when it reaches a limit. Those questions are more informative than a single impressive clip.

The episode's broader AI discussion also applies beyond cars. A model can perform one component well while the complete system still needs careful integration, evaluation, and recovery behavior. Understanding the whole task helps you appreciate the progress without assuming that success in one part settles the reliability of every other part.
