# From Zero Experience to $400K: Jennifer Gaddis' QA Story

Jennifer Gaddis, founder of Road to QA, discusses entering technology without prior experience and building a career that can support a different way of living. For a beginner, the most valuable opening is that technical contribution includes investigating software, not only writing its first version.

QA asks whether a product behaves as intended under the circumstances people will actually encounter. That can mean an empty form, a slow connection, an unexpected sequence of clicks, or an account with different permissions. Curiosity becomes useful when you turn it into a precise observation.

## Write a bug report another person can use

“Checkout is broken” tells a team that someone is unhappy. A stronger report names the environment, the steps taken, the expected result, the actual result, and any evidence that helps reproduce the problem.

Try it with an imaginary shop: add an item, apply a discount, remove the item, and inspect the total. If the discount remains attached to an empty cart, describe exactly when that happened. You are already practicing the difference between spotting something odd and helping a team diagnose it.

The income in the title belongs to a particular career story; it should not become an expectation for every entrant. Listen for the work behind the outcome: learning the product, communicating clearly, improving technical fluency, and earning trust through reliable investigation. Those are capabilities a learner can begin developing now.
