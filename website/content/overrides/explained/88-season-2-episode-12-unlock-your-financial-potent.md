# Season 2 Episode 12 Unlock Your Financial Potential: Expert Strategies with Jasmine Baker

Jasmine Baker's discussion of credit education gives learners a chance to replace a mysterious number with a clearer picture of the system producing it. Start with the distinction between the record and the score.

The [Consumer Financial Protection Bureau explains](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-the-difference-between-a-credit-report-and-a-credit-score-en-2069/) that a credit report contains information about credit activity and history, while a score is calculated using information from a report. Different models and underlying information can produce different scores. A number shown in one place is therefore not necessarily the exact number used in every decision.

## Understand the evidence before chasing the number

Think of a score as the output of a process. To understand a change, you need to know which information and which model are involved. A promise to improve a number quickly is less informative than an explanation of the specific issue being addressed.

For a learner, useful questions include what is recorded, whether it is accurate, and how a particular lender or service uses it. The answers help separate general education from claims about a guaranteed result for an individual.

This conversation belongs in a technology course because people encounter consequential systems through simplified interfaces every day. Learning to ask what information sits behind the output is a form of literacy that travels well beyond credit. It helps you become a more informed user of systems that otherwise feel like sealed boxes.
