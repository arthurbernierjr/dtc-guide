# Stedman Waiters: Building a Business Around Funding Other People's Businesses

Stedman Waiters brings the perspective of building a business around helping other businesses obtain capital. That changes the usual founder story: instead of asking only how to fund your own idea, the conversation asks what expertise and relationships allow you to help someone else move forward.

The work begins with a concrete need. A business might be buying equipment, covering a gap between delivery and payment, or opening another location. Those situations have different timelines and different consequences if the plan takes longer than expected.

## Explain the whole arrangement

A useful funding conversation should make the amount, costs, repayment expectations, and responsibilities understandable. It should also make the intermediary's role and compensation clear. Approval is a milestone; the business still has to operate successfully under the arrangement.

For a concrete example of why repayment matters, the [SBA's 7(a) loan overview](https://www.sba.gov/loans/7a-loans/) includes the borrower's ability to repay among its requirements and explains repayment through business cash flow. That is one program's structure, not a description of every funding product discussed in the interview.

Listen for how a funding business earns trust before and after an introduction. The durable service is helping a client understand a suitable path, provide accurate information, and evaluate the commitments involved. Capital can expand what a business can attempt, but the quality of the underlying plan still matters.
