# Jarrett Albritton: Big Tech Sales Energy The Secret Tech Job Nobody Talks About

Jarrett Albritton's Big Tech Energy conversation widens the picture of a technology career. Software companies need people who can connect technical capabilities with customer needs, explain tradeoffs, and coordinate decisions across organizations. Coding is one route into that world; it is not the only kind of expertise the work requires.

Someone with experience in retail, hospitality, or another customer-facing role may already know how to listen, handle uncertainty, and explain an unfamiliar offer. Those strengths become more valuable when paired with knowledge of the product and the customer's environment.

## Discovery comes before the demonstration

Imagine selling scheduling software to a clinic. A feature tour might show calendars, reminders, and reports. A discovery conversation asks why appointments are missed, who updates availability, which systems staff already use, and what would make a new tool difficult to adopt.

The answers change the demonstration. They may also reveal that the product is a poor fit. Recognizing that mismatch is part of credible sales work.

The large deal figures associated with a career can attract attention, but they do not explain the daily craft. Listen for how relationships, preparation, and product knowledge support the result. For a learner, a strong starting exercise is to explain one tool in terms of a real customer's work, then identify what you would need to ask before recommending it.
