# What They're Saying About AI in PropTech /w Liberty Madison

Liberty Madison's PropTech conversation offers a focused entry into the overlap between AI and a domain full of consequential, information-heavy work. The opportunity is more specific than adding a chatbot to a property website.

Consider the difference between helping someone find information, interpreting that information, and making a recommendation. Each step asks more of the system. An answer can sound confident while depending on incomplete, outdated, or misunderstood material.

## Give the user a way to inspect the answer

Imagine an assistant helping a property manager review maintenance records. A useful answer might summarize recurring issues while linking to the underlying reports. The manager can then check whether the pattern reflects the records and decide what action is warranted.

That design makes the tool's contribution clearer. It helps organize evidence while preserving a path back to the facts. If a record is missing or ambiguous, the interface should make that visible rather than filling the gap with plausible prose.

As you watch, choose one proposed application and ask whose work improves, what information it depends on, and how the user can catch a mistake. Those questions connect founder storytelling with product substance. A persuasive AI pitch becomes a stronger business proposition when the team can show how the experience supports a real decision in the customer's day.
