# Season 2 Episode 10: Liberty Madison's Formula for Founder Success & Investor Appeal

Liberty Madison's conversation links storytelling, user acquisition, and investor appeal. Those subjects belong together because a persuasive narrative creates expectations that the business must be able to support.

“People need this” is an opening claim. A sharper explanation identifies which people, what they currently do, why that approach is inadequate, and what evidence suggests they will adopt the alternative. The pitch becomes easier to follow when those ideas form a clear sequence.

## Make the growth story visible

Imagine a tool for independent property managers. A founder can describe a large real estate market, but the first acquisition question is much narrower: how will the next relevant property manager discover and evaluate the tool?

A credible answer might involve a specific professional community, a partnership, or a repeatable outreach process. The important part is explaining why the channel fits the customer and what the team has learned from trying it. Interest, a trial, and continued use are different kinds of evidence.

The interview's discussion of funding stages adds context, but no universal pitch formula removes the need to understand the particular business. Use the conversation to inspect your own explanation. Can another person connect the problem, the product, the route to customers, and the evidence without filling in missing steps? A clear story makes those connections inspectable; it cannot replace them.
