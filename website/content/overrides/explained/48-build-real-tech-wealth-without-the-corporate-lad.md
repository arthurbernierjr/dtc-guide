# Build Real Tech Wealth Without the Corporate Ladder

The invitation to look beyond the corporate ladder is valuable because “working in tech” is often pictured as one sequence of titles inside one kind of company. In practice, someone might build software for clients, sell a focused product, teach a difficult skill, or connect technical capabilities with a customer's business problem.

These paths are not interchangeable. Employment can offer structure and colleagues. Independent work adds responsibility for finding customers and managing delivery. A product adds the challenge of repeatedly serving people who may never speak to you directly.

## Choose a route you can investigate

Start by identifying a problem you understand well enough to discuss with the people who experience it. Then ask how they currently pay to solve it. That conversation reveals whether your next useful step is technical practice, domain knowledge, a portfolio example, or learning how to sell and support an offer.

A strong skill becomes more economically useful when someone can recognize its value. Clear explanations, reliable delivery, and evidence of completed work help bridge that gap.

The [Village route](/path/11-1-rallying-the-villagers/) offers a modest version of this ambition: make something useful for reachable people and understand what it costs to keep alive. A small, honest business model is a better starting point than a grand identity with no customer attached.
