# Exclusive Interview: The Missing Economic Strategy They Don't Want Discussed

The conversation with Dr. Umar connects economic inequality, the African diaspora, collective action, and the long horizon of generational wealth. It invites a different scale of thinking from the usual question of how one individual can earn more.

An institution carries work across individual lifetimes. A school preserves a teaching mission. A business can develop workers and serve recurring needs. A community organization can coordinate efforts that would be difficult for isolated people to sustain. The shared challenge is turning a strongly felt purpose into something that continues to function.

## Give solidarity an operating plan

Suppose a group wants to help young people enter technical careers. Agreement that the goal matters is a beginning. They still need teachers, a realistic budget, access to equipment, a way to assess learning, and people responsible for maintaining the program. They also need a way for participants to challenge decisions when the program falls short.

Those details deserve attention because accountability helps collective work survive disappointment and disagreement. A mission becomes more credible when people can inspect both its promises and its results.

While watching, separate historical arguments, proposed actions, and claims about future outcomes. Ask what each proposal would require from the people involved. That approach leaves room for strong convictions while making the practical work visible: building trust, developing capability, and creating organizations that can keep serving their communities.
