# How Close Are We to Escaping Earth?

Space exploration is irresistible partly because ordinary language makes enormous journeys sound close together. “Going to space” can mean reaching orbit, visiting another planet, or imagining travel between stars. Each destination changes the distance, the journey, and the support a mission would need.

NASA's discussion of [interstellar propulsion](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20230018678) makes the scale vivid: a spacecraft can travel an extraordinary distance by human standards while still requiring tens of thousands of years to cover the distance to a nearby star at Voyager-like speeds. Reaching interstellar space is different from arriving at another star system.

## Pack for the journey you actually mean

Imagine planning a voyage as a game master. It is tempting to start with the destination's wonders. The harder worldbuilding concerns the time in between: energy, maintenance, supplies, communication, and what happens when the crew encounters a problem without nearby help.

That exercise is useful precisely because it exposes assumptions hidden by a dramatic launch. A mission needs a way to keep functioning after the opening scene ends.

While watching, sort ideas into demonstrated capabilities, engineering work still required, and speculative concepts. A proposal can be fascinating without being a practical travel plan. Keeping those categories distinct lets you appreciate the ambition of exploration while understanding why one successful step does not remove every obstacle on the longer journey.
