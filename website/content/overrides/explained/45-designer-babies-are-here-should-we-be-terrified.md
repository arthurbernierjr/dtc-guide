# Designer Babies Are Here. Should We Be Terrified?

Editing a person's cells to address disease, testing embryos, and making changes intended to be inherited are different activities. They involve different mechanisms, uncertainties, and people affected by the decision. Treating them as one capability makes it difficult to say exactly what we are celebrating or questioning.

The [WHO's overview of human genome editing](https://www.who.int/health-topics/human-genome-editing) distinguishes somatic and germline applications. That distinction is a useful foundation for the episode's larger debate about treatment, enhancement, and the power to shape future lives.

## Who gets to define an improvement?

A decision about reducing suffering can raise different questions from a market promising preferred traits. Even an apparently desirable intervention invites questions about access, uncertainty, and who bears consequences they could not choose.

Science fiction often turns this into a world divided by engineered advantage. The real intellectual work is identifying the steps that could produce such a division: prices, institutional choices, social pressure, and the meanings attached to particular traits.

Use the episode to practice making a precise ethical argument. Name the intervention, identify who is affected, and explain which value guides your position. Two people can agree that technology should help children while disagreeing about what help permits. Understanding that disagreement is more valuable than letting a frightening or exciting label decide the answer.
