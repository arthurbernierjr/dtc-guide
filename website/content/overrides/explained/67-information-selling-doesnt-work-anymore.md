# Information Selling Doesn't Work Anymore

The challenge to information selling is worth taking seriously because access to explanations has expanded enormously. A buyer can often find definitions, tips, and examples without purchasing another collection of them. That changes what a useful educational offer needs to provide.

Information still matters. The question is what surrounds it. A beginner may not know which explanation fits their situation, which prerequisite they lack, or why an apparently reasonable attempt failed. More material can increase that confusion if it arrives without a learning path.

## Sell a better learning experience

Picture someone learning APIs. A glossary explains endpoints and requests. A carefully designed lesson also gives them a small task, shows a complete exchange, asks them to predict a result, and helps them interpret a common error. Feedback reveals whether they understood the idea or merely recognized the vocabulary.

That is a useful standard for this course too. A page should earn the learner's time through clarity, an illuminating example, or a next step they can actually attempt. Adding another paragraph is worthwhile when it changes what the reader can understand or do.

Watch the episode with one educational product in mind. Where does it remove uncertainty? Where does it support practice? Where does it leave the learner alone at the hardest moment? Those questions identify opportunities to improve the experience rather than simply repackage the information.
