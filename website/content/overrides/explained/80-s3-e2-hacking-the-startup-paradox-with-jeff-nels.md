# S3 E2 Hacking the Startup Paradox with Jeff Nelson Co-Founder of Blavity & Afrotech

Jeff Nelson's conversation draws on his work with Blavity, AfroTech, and Cinchapi, alongside his book Hacking the Startup Paradox. That range makes the interview a chance to think about the tensions involved in building an organization, rather than simply collecting startup slogans.

One tension to bring to the discussion is the relationship between belief and evidence. A founder has to act before every uncertainty is resolved. Yet refusing to revise a plan can turn commitment into an expensive attachment to an assumption.

## Decide what an experiment is allowed to change

Suppose a team believes a particular group needs a new collaboration tool. A prototype receives interest, but people repeatedly use only one small feature. The team could dismiss that behavior as misunderstanding, or investigate whether it points toward a more specific need.

The mission may remain important while the product changes. Making that distinction gives the team room to learn without treating every revision as surrender.

Listen to how Nelson describes the startup paradox and compare it with the decisions in your own project. His discussion of Black leadership also invites attention to whose problems receive investment and whose experience shapes the products being built. Those choices influence which opportunities become visible in the first place.
