# Season 2 Episode 20: Bootcamps and Mentorship

Bootcamps and mentorship promise structure in a field where a beginner can easily collect resources without knowing whether they are progressing. The useful question is how that structure changes what happens during a difficult week of learning.

Imagine reaching a project task you cannot complete. A list of videos offers more explanations. A responsive mentor may help you locate the specific gap: perhaps you understand functions but cannot yet trace how data moves between them. That diagnosis can turn a vague feeling of being bad at coding into a problem you can practice.

## Look for the feedback loop

Evaluate a learning experience through the work it asks learners to do and the response they receive. Are projects small enough to understand but substantial enough to expose misconceptions? Does feedback explain the reasoning behind a correction? Can a learner try again and see whether the understanding improved?

Mentorship also works better when the learner brings evidence. A clear description of the goal, the attempt, and the point of confusion gives a mentor something concrete to examine. Learning to ask that kind of question is itself a professional skill.

As you watch, picture the support you would need at your next obstacle. A compelling outcome story is encouraging; the day-to-day process should explain how the program helps a person become more independent, rather than merely better at following the instructor's next instruction.
