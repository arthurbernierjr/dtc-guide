# Who Needs Iron Man? Power in Unity & Votes!

The Avengers analogy turns collective action into something easy to picture. Individual strengths become more useful when the team can coordinate them. But a real community also has to answer questions superhero stories can resolve with a dramatic speech: who gets a vote, how proposals reach the agenda, and who does the work afterward?

Those questions matter in a local organization, a creator community, or a decentralized project. Technical voting tools can record a decision. They do not automatically make participation broad or the decision well informed.

## Follow a proposal past the vote

Imagine a community choosing how to spend its shared fund. Someone writes a proposal, others need time and information to consider it, and a person or group must implement the result. Members then need to see what happened to the money and whether the promised work was completed.

Influence exists at every stage. A large audience can shape the conversation before voting begins, while a small group of reliable contributors may control what is realistically deliverable.

The episode offers a useful starting point for thinking beyond the lone hero. Try designing one modest decision process for a group you belong to. A clear proposal, a fair chance to respond, and a visible result can make collective power feel real without waiting for a perfect governance system.
