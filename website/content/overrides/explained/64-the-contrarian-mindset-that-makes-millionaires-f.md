# The Contrarian Mindset That Makes Millionaires | Ft. Jared T. Ross

Jared T. Ross's conversation explores differentiation, loyalty, and the appeal of a brand willing to take a clear position. That can be powerful when the position expresses a real conviction about how a customer should be served.

Being provocative is easier than being distinctive. A statement may attract argument without giving anyone a lasting reason to buy or return. The stronger test is whether the point of view changes what the business makes, how it behaves, or whom it serves.

## Put the disagreement into the offer

Imagine a fitness business that rejects punishing, all-or-nothing routines. Its alternative should appear in the program: shorter sessions, recovery plans, and a way to resume after missing a week. The belief becomes credible through the experience.

That choice also creates a boundary. Someone seeking a different style of training may prefer another provider. A focused brand can accept that without treating outsiders as enemies or turning its community into a loyalty test.

While watching, identify the conventional assumption being challenged and the alternative being offered. Then ask what evidence would show that the alternative serves its intended audience better. The aim is a point of view with consequences. A customer should be able to encounter the product and understand the position even if they have never seen the founder's most attention-grabbing post.
