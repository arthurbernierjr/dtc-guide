# Bitcoin's Biggest Halving Meets AI Singularity in 2032

This episode places a Bitcoin halving and an anticipated AI turning point around the same future horizon. The juxtaposition raises an interesting question: what happens when changes in money, computing, and people's expectations interact?

The mechanisms are different. Bitcoin's subsidy schedule is tied to block height: the subsidy halves every 210,000 blocks, while the calendar date is an estimate. An AI “singularity” is a much more speculative proposition about technological capability and its consequences. One does not become a scheduled event because it shares a year with the other. The [Bitcoin halving explanation](https://bitcoin.org/en/halving) provides the protocol side of that distinction.

## Follow the causal connection

To turn a broad forecast into an argument, fill in the steps. How might more capable software affect production, employment, demand for computing, or financial behavior? Which of those effects would change demand for a particular asset? Where could the chain fail?

This exercise preserves the ambition of the conversation while making it possible to examine. It also separates a supply rule from a price prediction: a known issuance change does not determine future demand.

Keep two columns while watching: mechanisms you can explain and outcomes being forecast. The most valuable notes are often the arrows between them, because those arrows reveal what evidence would strengthen or weaken the larger thesis.
